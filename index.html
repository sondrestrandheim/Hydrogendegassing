About hydrogen in aluminium

Hydrogen is the only gas that is soluble in aluminium. The solubility is much higher in liquid aluminium than in solid aluminium. If the hydrogen content is too high in the melt, porosity will form during solidification.

Typical requirements

For DC-cast products: < 0.15–0.20 ml H₂/100g Al


Hydrogen source

The main source of hydrogen is moisture in the surrounding air or in refractory material. Water vapour reacts with liquid aluminium:

3H₂O(g) + 2Al(l) → Al₂O₃(s) + 3H₂(g)

Some of this hydrogen dissolves as atoms in the melt:

H₂(g) → 2[H] (dissolved in Al)

Sievert's Law

The solubility of diatomic gases in liquid metals is calculated using Sievert's Law:

S = KT · Calloy · √PH₂

where:

KT = K₀ · exp(-ΔH / RT)

Constants (Anyalebechi, 2022):

K₀ [ml/100g]419,867,14

Water vapour saturation in air

The amount of water vapour that can be absorbed by air is highly temperature dependent. At room temperature, the saturation is 17.3 g/m³ (~14,300 ppm water, ~1,600 ppm H₂). Higher temperature = more moisture = more hydrogen available to react with the melt.

Pressure effect on solubility

According to Sievert's Law, solubility is proportional to √PH₂. Lower pressure (e.g. at higher altitude or vacuum treatment) gives lower solubility, which drives hydrogen out of the melt.






Alloy effect on solubility

Alloying elements affect hydrogen solubility through the correction factor Calloy:

Calloy = ∏(1 + ei·wt%i + ri·wt%i²)

Argon degassing simulation

This simulation shows how argon bubbles are injected at the bottom of the melt, rise upward, and absorb hydrogen along the way. The bubbles grow due to lower hydrostatic pressure and uptake of H₂. Hydrogen is transported from the melt into the bubbles driven by the difference between equilibrium partial pressure and actual partial pressure in the bubble.

Pressure in the system

Atmospheric pressure:

Patm = 101 325 Pa (1 atm)

Metallostatic pressure (pressure from the weight of liquid Al above the bubble):

Pmet(h) = ρAl · g · (H - h)

Total pressure at depth h from surface:

Ptotal(h) = Patm + ρAl · g · h

where ρAl = 2350 kg/m³, g = 9.81 m/s², H = melt depth

Example: At 0.8 m depth: Pbottom = 101 325 + 2350·9.81·0.8 = 119 770 Pa (1.18 atm)

Bubble expansion & mass transport

Ideal gas law → bubble volume expands:

V(h) = V₀ · Pbunn / P(h)
d(h) = d₀ · (Pbunn / P(h))1/3

Driving force for H₂ transport into bubble:

ΔC = [H]melt − KT·√PH₂,bubble

Fresh argon bubble: PH₂ ≈ 0 → large driving force.
As the bubble absorbs H₂, PH₂ in the bubble increases and the driving force decreases.

Rise velocity (Mendelson):

v = √(2σ/(ρ·d) + g·d/2)

σ = 0.87 N/m (surface tension of liquid Al)
